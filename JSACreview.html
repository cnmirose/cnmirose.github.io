JSAC Special Issue on Molecular, Biological, and Multi-Scale Communications


TCP-Like Molecular Communications:

In this paper, we present a communication protocol between biological nanomachines built upon molecular communications in an aqueous environment. In our proposal, a biological nanomachine, acting as both receiver and control node, sends a setup signal to the transmitter, which is another biological nanomachine which stokes molecules, to start molecules transmission. They propagate in the environment and chemically reacts with the receiver by binding with its receptors. When the desired quantity of molecules has been absorbed by the receiver, it notifies the transmitter to stop the transmission. The proposed protocol implements a bidirectional communication by using a number of techniques originally designed for the TCP protocol. In fact, the proposed protocol is connection oriented, and uses the TCP network probing feature to select the optimal transmission rate between transmitter and receiver. However, due to the large delay which characterizes molecular communications, using explicit acknowledgments is not a suitable solution, since it would shrink the communication throughput. Thus, acknowledgments are implicit, and feedback messages are only negative, i.e. they are sent to throttle the transmission rate at the transmitter. We also present the results of an extensive simulation campaign, used to validate the proposed solution and to properly dimension the main protocol parameters.

Channel Noise Models for Nonlinear Molecular Communication Systems:

Recently, a tabletop molecular communication plat- form has been developed for transmitting short text messages across a room. The end-to-end system impulse response for this platform does not follow previously published theoretical works because of imperfect receiver, transmitter, and turbulent flows. Moreover, it is observed that this platform resembles a nonlinear system, which makes the rich body of theoretical work that has been developed by communication engineers not applicable to this platform. In this work, we first introduce corrections to the previous theoretical models of the end-to- end system impulse response based on the observed data from experimentation. Using the corrected impulse response models, we then formulate the nonlinearity of the system as noise and show that through simplifying assumptions it can be represented as Gaussian noise. Through formulating the system's nonlinearity as the output a linear system corrupted by noise, the rich toolbox of mathematical models of communication systems, most of which are based on linearity assumption, can be applied to this platform.


Performance of Concentration-Encoded Molecular Communication Based on Anomalous Diffusion:

In this paper, the effects of anomalous diffusion on Concentration-Encoded Molecular Communication (CEMC) have been investigated. Although classical (i.e. normal) diffusion is a widely used model of diffusion in molecular communication (MC) research, anomalous diffusion is quite common in biological media involving bionanomachines, yet inadequately addressed as a research issue in MC literature so far. In this paper, using fractional diffusion approach, an investigative analysis has been made in order to study the performance of CEMC system based on anomalous diffusion between a pair of nanomachines. Molecular propagation effects in anomalous diffusion in unbounded three-dimensional propagation medium have been shown. Correspondingly, its effects on CEMC are investigated from the viewpoints of temporal dispersion parameters of channel impulse response (CIR) of CEMC channel and bit error rate (BER) performance. CIR of CEMC channel in anomalous diffusion process is found to behave differently compared to that in normal diffusion-based propagation. It is found that anomalous diffusion has distinctive time-dispersive properties that should be taken into account when designing a CEMC system based on anomalous diffusion. In addition, in order to detect information symbols in CEMC channel in anomalous diffusion process, the receiver needs larger memory size to operate correctly, compared to normal diffusion process, thereby requiring more complex receiver structure. An in-depth analysis has been made to show the performance of the receiver under several scenarios when communication range, transmission data rate, and memory size vary.

Error Probabilities in Bipolar Nano-Networks:

Nano-networks that communicate using particles, molecules or action potentials generally have a very simple data encoding such as electrons with bipolar spin, molecules with distinct chirality, and excitatory or inhibitory action potentials in neuronal and dendritic networks. Such signals cannot identify the origin and destination of messages, and are subject to interference by other similar signals, misdirection of to the wrong ``receiver'', and random changes in polarity. Thus they can easily contain errors. Since the conventional ``signal plus noise'' model of communication theory does not apply to such systems, we develop an error analysis for sources that communicate with flows of particles or point signals that can be corrupted by loss, interference and random polarity changes. We also suggest a detector based on bipolar counters, similar to two integrate and fire neurons, one of which increments its state with arriving positive polarity and decrements it with negative polarity signals, and the other having the opposite behaviour, with identical random decay which limits their memory span. If the incoming particles are split equally at the detector between the two counters which are combined for decision making, we show that the effect of noise can be substantially eliminated when the decay rate of the counters' state matches the rate at which signals arrive to the detectors. In the particular case of spin communications, we also discuss the effect of entangled particles. The energy consumption per correctly received particle is also discussed.
