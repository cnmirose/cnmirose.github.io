At WINLAB, we’ve spent a number of years working on the problem  of delivering giant files through radio systems.  When we say “giant”,  we’re talking about files a thousand times bigger than the largest email attachment you would ever try to send today-- files like a DVD movie, for example.  Even with the most optimistic assumptions for the next generation of wireless systems, such a download would take 10-30 hours and cost a ridiculous amount.   And it isn’t just wireless.  Using DSL to your home, it would still take 10-20 hours to download a DVD movie.  Others are taking the route of using lower quality formats to deliver movies to the home over DSL, but we believe that with large screens TVs becoming so popular, and with high definition coming, quality will become increasingly important.  In thinking about it, we realized that there was a technology and method that could allow us to deliver a variety of giant files, including not only movies but TV programming, music, software and local information as well, and that we could simultaneously serve the home and mobile markets.
The technology is a surprising one-- the magnetic hard disk such as we have in every PC and laptop.  It’s an amazing technology that has a better learning curve than the famous Moore’s law for chips.  It’s capacity has doubled every year for more than a decade, while it has grown smaller and faster and cheaper.  It’s a tremendously competitive market-- companies have names like “dirt cheap drives,” and advertise “blowout sales”.  Within a couple of years, a hard disk that fits in you shirt pocket will hold several DVD movies and cost less than $100.  You will be able to load a DVD into it in 15-30 seconds. That same technology will be available in larger packages having many disks, that will cost only a few thousand dollars and store thousands of movies-- and every year this technology will keep getting better.  The question was how to build an exciting system and service out of this technology.  The answer is that we use these storage devices not only to to store the giant files, but to move them around as well.  This physical movement of the content is much faster and much cheaper than the wireline and radio alternatives.  It allows us to offer a virtually unlimited menu of content to the customer, at very low cost.
We’ve been calling our system Zappster.  It starts with the customer who caries a small terminal that we call a personal data pack or PDP. He fills it at a network of Zappster kiosks that offer a large selection of the most popular content.  The kiosks only cost about $10,000, but combine the features of a video rental store and music store and book store, plus content like TV programs that is currently available only as real-time broadcast.   He then views or listens to that content through a variety of “players”, such as home TVs, laptops and palmtops, so Zappster provides entertainment and information for both the home and mobile markets. Even for movies alone, this method offers many advantages compared to the most similar alternative-- the video rental store.  It’s just a kiosk-- no building, no inventory, no employees, no returns, yet nothing is ever out of stock. It even solves the DVD encryption problem.   It serves the home market while developing the new market for mobile entertainment and information.  We’re particularly excited by the idea of our kiosk as a new distribution channel for TV programming.  You can download your favorite shows, and shows you missed, and old shows you want to see again.  And all this content is delivered to individual customers with known profiles, so it can be delivered with more valuable, targeted advertising. By now you may have noticed that we have gone from movies to a virtually open-ended menu.  You may be wondering how we plan to put all that in a kiosk– even one that doubles its capacity every year.  The answer is that we put only the most popular content into the kiosk-- say a thousand movies and a thousand hours of TV, for example.  This will serve most of the requests– and we can get the rest there on demand, using special orders and what we call our “hybrid network.”
The Zappster hybrid network includes a service center, where a really huge amount of content is stored.  Special orders arrive at the service center website, and are automatically scheduled and confirmed for deliver at a particular kiosk at a particular time.  For example, special orders placed by early afternoon could be available for pickup by the evening commute. The special order content is then downloaded to transportable disk packs, which are physically delivered to the kiosks on a regular scheduled route.  In addition, DSL lines to the kiosks can be used for Internet deliver of smaller files, such as stock quotes and personal messages. The capacity of this physical delivery channel is so large that it is virtually infinite when compared to DSL or wireless options, and the cost is essentially independent of the amount delivered.  In addition to the special orders, this physical delivery network will handle updates of the kiosk menu, including new movies and network programs. 
In summary, we have discussed the problem of delivering giant files and proposed a new method. We have shown that while video rental is a effective technique, Zappster offers powerful advantages.  It is cheaper, better serves a mobile population, and can deliver many new forms of content. We have shown how magnetic storage technology is the key to a large menu of content in the kiosk, an almost infinite menu on special order through a hybrid network, and a small, low-cost PDP.
We think the costs look attractive.
Looking out a few more years, we think that Zappster can evolve into a dramatic new type of 2-way global network for giant files.  We can achieve this by interconnecting our service centers through low-cost, high-density fiber routes, and using our local hybrid network to complete the delivery at low cost, where fiber isn’t available. We can even extend our network beyond the kiosk, and deliver and pick up files at the home. What is particularly exciting is that this is a proven technology and the initial market is pretty well understood.  Thus we can get started quickly, with little risk, based on existing technology and markets, which evolving gradually into a more complete global network
