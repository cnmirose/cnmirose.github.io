Sending “Giant” Files
3G dreams & Cost Realities
Several years ago, we became convinced that the wireless systems of today, including the third generation systems being proposed for the coming decade, were not suitable for the very large file sizes that we want for information and entertainment.  This fact has been masked by talk of high data rates like 384 Kilobits per second, or even the megabits per second that such systems may offer under optimum conditions. Combined with compression techniques that can reduce music or movie to smaller files, they conjure up an image of delivering streaming audio and even video, to people on the move.  What they don’t say is that while these systems are marginally fast enough to deliver a reduced quality of video, the cost per delivered bit will remain stubbornly high.  People who were turned on by an Internet that is essentially free are going to be shocked by the toll charges on the wireless superhighway.    Video and music to our cellphones may be technologically feasible, but it is not economically viable.
Does this matter?  One could argue that email and some web-based services are all we need in our cell phones.  We think this flies in the face of what is happening out there.  We look out there and see content in ever richer forms.  Movies are going to DVD formats and will go beyond that to high definition.  Software packagers get bigger.  Games get more elaborate.  More music videos are produced.  More channels of TV programming are released.  Laptops and palms play movies and music.  And people on the move have special needs for information-- they need maps, images and detailed information about their surroundings.  Yet the inadequacies of wireless are leading to a world in which all that content is denied to people on the move, where they spend so much of their lives.
