Personal favorites

Papers I am still fond of

  • 1990 A Dielectric-Free Superconducting Coaxial Cable IEEE Trans. MTT 38(2), with Mike Gans. The idea is to levitate the center conductor and thereby avoid a lossy dielectric support. Conceived during the euphoria after the discovery of high-Tc superconductors. Current densities are still too low for this to be feasible, but it was fun to dream.
  • 1989 Rapid Optimal Scheduling for Time-Multiplex Switches Using a Cellular Automaton IEEE Trans. Comm. 37(5). Let a set of autonomous, simple, simply-connected computers rapidly calculate "diagonals" — also called systems of distinct representatives — for a given offered traffic matrix.
  • 1992 Mean Internodal Distance in Multihop Store & Forward Networks IEEE Trans. Comm. 40(8). This paper and its sequel were a response to the "network of the week" craze that was driving me crazy. The basic point: random networks do very well on connectivity and on mean hop distance — a measure of how efficient a network is.
  • 1987 The Performance of Random and Optimal Scheduling in a Time-Multiplex Switch IEEE Trans. Comm. 35(8). My very first paper EVER. Written with Mike Hluchyj, later founder of Sonus Networks.
  • 2004 Inscribed Matter as an Efficient Means of Communication with an Extraterrestrial Civilization Nature 431, pp. 47–49, with Gregory Wright. Plus supplementary information and the whole cosmic communications story.
  • 2026 Limits of Information Flow Between Classically Interacting Particles Physical Review E, with Miles Miller-Dickson. The newest one, and possibly the beginning of the rest of it.

Declined proposals, posted as a public service

I submitted an NSF proposal that was declined, but the reviews were especially helpful. As it goes with these things, one often feels the reviewer might have missed the point — see the comment about characterizing moving foliage being infeasible. I agree, but if so, that still tells you something about the channel estimation problem. Objectively though, the reviewer may also be pointing out that I missed the point.

Anyone who wants to run with the idea, or deride it, is welcome. I am thinking of posting all proposals and subsequent reviews here — possibly helpful to new faculty, if only as an example of what not to do if you want to get funded.
