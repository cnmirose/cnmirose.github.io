The Creation of Knowledge
Creating knowledge and transmitting it to present and future generations is the very essence of a research university. At Rutgers, research programs take this one step further; our students, both undergraduate and graduate, are actively involved in research, which prepares them to become tomorrow's innovators.

Science in the News

New Milestones on the Path to Conquering HIV Drug Resistance
HIV is a moving target, unpredictibly changing direction to elude anti-AIDS drugs, but researchers at Rutgers are clearly on the track of solutions to combat HIV drug resistance.


Extraterrestrial Communication
Inscribing information and physically sending it to some location in deep space is more energy-efficient that pulsing it out on radio waves, which disperse as they travel, according to one Rutgers researcher.
Tracking Hudson River Water in the Atlantic
Marine scientists have released a nontoxic red dye into the Atlantic Ocean off New Jersey to help reveal the contents and fate of Hudson River water after it joins the Atlantic.
Can a Plant that Acts Like Poison Ivy Cure Prostate Cancer?
The croton plant, pictured at left, has long been known to oriental herbalists and homeopaths; an oil in its seeds shows promise for the treatment of prostate cancer.
$30 Million for the Protein Data Bank
The Protein Data Bank, one of the world's most critical resources for solving the mysteries of human disease, has received a commitment of a record $30 million in federal support for the next five years.
Research to Safeguard U.S. Food Supply
Rutgers Researcher Thomas Montville is gearing up to investigate ways of ensuring that U.S. processed food is protected from anthrax. He will explore ways to protect foods as they pass from the farm to the retail counter.
Protein Function May Hold Key to Learning and Memory Disorders
A research group has discovered what could be the newest target for drugs in the treatment of memory and learning disabilities as well as diseases such as Alzheimer's and fetal alcohol syndrome.
A Gateway for Wireless Internet Testing
Ninety years ago, radio pioneer Guglielmo Marconi’s New Brunswick station along Easton Avenue made the Rutgers area a center of wireless research. It’s happening again, only this time it’s wireless Internet.
The Power of Speech
Phone numbers, remote controls and computer keyboards will seem like antiques in 10 years, according to Professor Lawrence Rabiner of Rutgers' Center for Advanced Information Processing.
Rutgers Targets Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan
Ilya Raskin of Rutgers' Biotechnology Center for Agriculture and the Environment is going hunting in Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan for plants, fungi and microbes with pharmaceutical potential.
