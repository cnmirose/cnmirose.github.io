The ET Might Write
Cartoon Contest

Judged, unreasonably, on physics

Students were invited to draw the central idea of the Nature paper: that a distant civilization would do better to post us a letter than to radio ahead. What nobody warned them was that the judge would evaluate the entries on whether they got the interstellar distances right.

The rubric, apparently: did the drawing convey that the sender is very far away, that the message is inscribed in matter, and that nobody is making a return trip? Artistic merit was noted, and then largely set aside.

Similar in spirit to Miro's drawing, though it did get the distance involved a little better. The ETs are sending their message from a ship, which negates the energy efficiency of inscribed-matter channels. It's also unclear why the human is saying "Life?" when the airplane has not hit him yet.
