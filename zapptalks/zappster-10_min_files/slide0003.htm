We’ve been calling our system Zappster.  It starts with the customer who caries a small terminal that we call a personal data pack or PDP. He fills it at a network of Zappster kiosks that offer a large selection of the most popular content.  The kiosks only cost about $10,000, but combine the features of a video rental store and music store and book store, plus content like TV programs that is currently available only as real-time broadcast.   He then views or listens to that content through a variety of “players”, such as home TVs, laptops and palmtops, so Zappster provides entertainment and information for both the home and mobile markets.
Even for movies alone, this method offers many advantages compared to the most similar alternative-- the video rental store.  It’s just a kiosk-- no building, no inventory, no employees, no returns, yet nothing is ever out of stock. It even solves the DVD encryption problem.   It serves the home market while developing the new market for mobile entertainment and information.  We’re particularly excited by the idea of our kiosk as a new distribution channel for TV programming.  You can download your favorite shows, and shows you missed, and old shows you want to see again.
And all this content is delivered to individual customers with known profiles, so it can be delivered with more valuable, targeted advertising.
By now you may have noticed that we have gone from movies to a virtually open-ended menu.  You may be wondering how we plan to put all that in a kiosk– even one that doubles its capacity every year.  The answer is that we put only the most popular content into the kiosk-- say a thousand movies and a thousand hours of TV, for example.  This will serve most of the requests– and we can get the rest there on demand, using special orders and what we call our “hybrid network.”
