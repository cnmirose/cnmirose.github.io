Certainly a study group is an intermediate goal, but it seems advisable to more carefully noodle about the overall structure of what we'd like to do and then figure out who to invite and set a careful agenda. The breadth of technical areas touched upon (communications, databases, distributed collaboration, security) and the nature of the "softer" issues (legal, social, political) will probably require a clear framework around which participants of widely different backgrounds can successfully rally (or rail).  That is, to interest the right smart folks and produce a useful output, there needs to be an intellectually deep and socially compelling theme  -- especially given the almost visceral public resistance to TIA.

So bluntly put, though the nature of the topic is sure to engender a fun, free-for-all out-of-the-box thinkfest, it must also produce concrete results that can be used for you to sculpt a program. Specifically, I'd like have a meeting which will help:

  1. Identify compelling and achievable goals for a high impact program on "volks intelligence" under your aegis.                                                                                                                                              
  2. Develop a network of gifted personnel which will make the program's  ultimate success unavoidable.

Are these attractive "deliverables" from your perspective?

If so, the only "bad" news is that doing this right  will require a significant amount of time on my part.  What are the necessary ingredients?  Read on if I've managed to hold your interest.

  • Background Work:   The project area demands an unusual combination of topical breadth and depth on the part of the organizer/moderator since participants necessarily will include folks from all over the intellectual landscape.  Thus, I'll need to acquire sufficient intellectual depth in requisite "hard" and "soft" fields.
  • Study Group Organization:   The background work will necessarily identify likely folks with whom I'll need to socialize ideas (via email and phone and perhaps a meeting or two in special cases).  As we sculpt the meeting agenda, the time-consuming work of obtaining actual participation commitments  can be handled by my clerical staff.  However, my experience organizing a variety of technical workshops and conferences has shown that careful and repeated discussions with participants are most effective in guaranteeing a successful meeting.
