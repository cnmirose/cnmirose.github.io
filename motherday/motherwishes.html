WHAT MOMS WANT: IN THEIR OWN WORDS

DO YOU REALLY want to know what moms want for Mother's Day? We canvassed our readers (who just happen to be mothers) and asked them to tell us what they'd really like for Mother's Day that wouldn't cost their families anything. Here's what they told us:

"I'd like my husband to spend some time with my son compiling a list of four things that I do for them that make their lives easier and four things that I do that make their lives more fun, and give me the list on Mother's Day. I'd feel valued knowing they notice the time I spend on them, and it'd help me to keep being innovative and efficient."

"A great gift would be to walk and feed the dogs for one whole day - without being asked or yelled at."

"What I would like [my daughter] to do for me on Mother's Day is to wake me up with a kiss and an 'I love you, Mom. Happy Mother's Day...' "

"My son....and my daughter...could give me the best Mother's Day present by talking politely to each other and not fighting for one whole day."

"Every Mother's Day I ask for the same presents - for everyone to leave the house and give me the day to myself! My husband takes the children out early in the morning, and they spend the day bike-riding, hiking or playing ball. Before they leave, they make me breakfast in bed, leave a full pot of coffee in the kitchen, and set up my lunch. I spend the day in my pajamas, reading a good novel, soaking in a bath and listening to music that wasn't written for an animated movie. My husband and kids return home early in the evening (about the time that I'm starting to miss them). They end the day by making me dinner. I look forward to the serenity of my Mother's Days; it beats going out to dinner in a crowded restaurant any day! And my children get to have a special day with their Dad, too."

"A lovely way to spend Mother's Day would be to spend the day with your family just relaxing, watch some movies together, play some games like Yahtzee together and maybe even have a picnic indoors."

"Create a memory album with the theme 'mother' or create a video where the children lip sync to a favorite song."

"Most important and cherished would be:
  • A really good day together that is preplanned by the children.
  • A real day off - everyone taking charge of my jobs. That means getting up before me at 8 a.m., doing the laundry (because I don't take Sundays off), sweeping the floor after a meal..."
  • A love note or a thank you note about being their mother. Telling me their hopes and dreams and some of the special times with me."
"The thing my family can do for me that doesn't cost anything is give me time:
  • Time to read something besides a book on raising your children better;
  • Time to watch a video that's not rated G or PG;
  • Time to plan and cook a meal more complicated than macaroni and cheese;
  • Time to shop for clothes - for me;
  • Time to see a movie that isn't a Disney movie;
  • Time to do nothing - absolutely nothing - and not feel guilty about it."
"I would love to have my car washed inside and out on Mother's Day."

"[Have Dad and the kids make] Mother's Day coupons that Mom can use when she needs a special moment to herself, with her children or husband. One coupon could say, 'I need two hours to myself, please' or 'I need to spend time with my husband,' or 'I would like to spend the day with my son.' Take it a step further: Coupons could be use for an evening out with friends or to hire someone to babysit while Mom either stays home to relax or dashes out of the house for a couple of hours."

"Let Mom sleep late."

"The children can take me to the [zoo] to view the animals."

"What I would really like for Mother's Day is breakfast in bed, then the opportunity to take a nice long bubble bath listening to classical music while the rest of the family is cooking our traditional Sunday lunch."

"I am the mother of a 1-year-old, so last year was my first Mother's Day. I didn't have high expectations for celebrating this day but I wanted to feel special. My husband is not big at celebrating birthdays, anniversaries or anything, so one week before Mother's Day he found a letter I wrote for him as if written by our then-10-month-old baby. In this letter I stated the plans for the day, the duties (diapers, lunch menu sort of pre-made), breakfast in bed with a flower on the tray, and a homemade card. The three of us had a wonderful day, and I am looking forward to writing a letter again this year!"

A special thank you to all of our readers who "talked to us" about Mother's Day and congratulations to Margaret Francis of Westchester County, N.Y., and Carol Burton and Therese L. of Connecticut, three mothers who won tickets for their families to New York Skyride.
