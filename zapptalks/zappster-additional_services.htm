Additional configurations/capabilities of the Zappster network

 

The basic Zappster configuration consists of an array of kiosks for the download of large files (movies, music, etc.) to portable terminals, using either a plug-in connection or a high-speed radio link (or possibly an infra-red link).  Frequently requested files are stored at the kiosk, while a virtually open-ended menu of additional content can be delivered to the kiosk within a short time, on demand, using the Zappster hybrid network.  This hybrid network combines the physical transportation of files to the kiosks with a more limited wireline capability such as DSL, so that very attractive combinations of delay and cost can be achieved, as required, for different types of content.

 

Inherent in the delivery of entertainment is the potential for competition from Video On Demand services, which seek to deliver content directly to the home.  Currently the highest speed that is widely available to the home is about 1 Megabit per second (DSL), which would offer a reduced quality of video compared to cable or DVD, but one may speculate that speeds of 5 Mbps will eventually become available, and will deliver DVD quality to the home on demand.  Such speeds would allow on-line viewing of DVD quality movies, so it is interesting to consider Zappster in this competitive context.

 

First, we note that even if  a DVD-quality capability (5 Mbps) were to become available at reasonable costs, it will not be available everywhere, and will serve only a single program at a time.  Zappster will achieve a very low price point, can download multiple selections in one session, and can easily adapt to higher quality programming, such as high-definition TV.   Moreover, for files that must be completely delivered to be useful (e.g., software, games, and location-dependent information) the concept of real-time viewing is not applicable, and the fastest Internet connects will require long download times compared to Zappster.  Finally, while VOD using wireline to the home will potentially serve a portion of the home entertainment market, Zappster will create a competing network that is widely available outside the home.  It will make large files available “everywhere” and while serving the home entertainment market, it will introduce an entirely new market for entertainment and information for people on the move.  It will extend entertainment and information options into this new environment, in the same way that cellular systems extended the use of telephony.

 

It has been shown (ref) that VOD would place a huge amount of new traffic on the Internet.  Specifically, we estimate that if only 15% of the current TV viewing were delivered in this way, the traffic would be 1000 times that which is currently carried on the Internet.  For this reason, it will be necessary to create caches at network points near the user.  These might be at local central offices, for example, or within a cable network at a point that serves a limited number of homes.  The low cost and high capacity of the Zappster kiosk suggests that it could be used in such a manner.  In this configuration, one might time share the high-speed download capability of the kiosk over a number of slower (VOD) lines to homes.  Alternatively, the kiosk could download the requested content to smaller caches (similar to the Zappster terminals), where each cache would then provide real-time video to a single home.

 

A significant advantage of this approach is that the kiosks can in turn be supplied with additional content through the Zappster hybrid network.  In this way, the large content of the kiosk cache becomes the open-ended content of the Zappster service center, available “on order” within a short time.  Compared to the public-access kiosk, the kiosk caches at central offices or within the cable TV network will have a higher speed wireline connection for downloading additional content, but the physical transportation of files will continue to play an important role.  As users select from a huge menu of old and new television programs, movies and music, the load is likely to exceed the capability of the wireline supply network.  The physical delivery capability of the Zappster hybrid network will provide an attractive cost point, and under heavy load may provide the earliest delivery as well.  Moreover, the scheduling software at the Zappster service center will make the best use of both the wireline and physical delivery options, and will allow orders to be confirmed for delivery at specific times.  Thus, the same service center can supply both public kiosks and kiosk cashes for DSL and cable delivery.

 

Yet another Zappster delivery option is created by the radio download capability of the kiosk.  It will be possible to install a kiosk in a delivery vehicle, and to download to Zappster terminals in homes over this radio link as the vehicle follows a “delivery route”.  Once again, this additional service would use the content, scheduling and customer interface capabilities of the Zappster service center.
