When you’re designing systems for a mass market, you know you’re on the right track when your technology is sold by companies called “Dirt Cheap Drives”, who advertise “Blow Out Sales”.  We can buy a 3-1/2 inch drive like this that will hold 5 DVDs for about $100 bucks, or one that will hold 2 DVDs for $70.
And they’ve gotten smaller as well. For PCs there was little need for hard disks to get smaller, but laptops required a smaller configuration, like this 2-1/2 inch 6 Gigabyte drive.  It can hold 1 1/2 DVDs, and in two years that will become 6 DVDs.  They’re even coming out as  PCMCIA cards with on board disks.  This one inch disk actually holds 1 Gigabyte --  a couple of music CDs or VCR quality movies .  In a couple of years, this will hold a DVD movie.  The capacity, cost and size performance of these drives is incredible.
Hard drives are also fast.  We will soon be able to write that DVD movie onto a hard disk in 15 seconds, or a VCR quality movie in 2 seconds.
In a world where we are used to impressive learning curves, the magnetic hard disk stands out.  It’s capacity doubles every year, and will can write to it twice as fast every two years, while its cost and size keep dropping.  This is the technology we want to ride.
