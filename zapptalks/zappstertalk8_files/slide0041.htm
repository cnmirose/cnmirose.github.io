And then-  The Zappster kiosk
The place you go for giant files
Then we looked at those Infostations scattered all around, and we realized that people on the move could access them today, without even using a radio, by simply plugging in their terminals.  The Infostation became the Zappster kiosk—sort of an ATM for information and entertainment where people could pause for a few seconds and “fill up.”  Instead of waiting for a few years while the radios got designed, the technology to make this happen exists today.   We can get started without radio, and we can use radio to make it better later on.
And finally, we realized that many of the same problems we had been working on exist for people at home as well as people on the move.  There’s not enough low cost capacity to the home either.  Cable doesn’t give us free choice of what we get, and internet access, even with cable modems and DSL, is too slow for giant files. Nothing gives us unlimited access to see what we want, when we want it-- at an affordable price.  We released that we could create a new kind of system system that would do it all-- deliver giant files at low cost— to people at home and people on the move.  It could make that sea of content available to everyone-- what they want, when they want, and where they want-- and at a price they will be willing to pay.  And our system evolves smoothly-- from a simple but exciting entertainment service, to a global network for moving giant entertainment and information files—anywhere.   The technology that makes it possible will surprise you.
That’s the story we want to tell you today.
